    This post is about “The algebra of classes”.

    This topic mainly covers theorems that will be useful for proving. Thus, the main contents will be theorems and their proofs.

    1.20 Theorem

    If \(A\) and \(B\) are any classes, then

      1. \(A \subseteq A \cup B\) and \(B \subseteq A \cup B\).
      1. \(A \cap B \subseteq A\) and \(B \cap A \subseteq B\).

    For proving, we need the relationships from the previous post: negation, conjunction, disjunction, and implication, along with their theorems. In particular, we use 1.5(i), 1.5(i)‘, 1.5(ii), and 1.5(ii)’.

    By Definition 1.13, \(x\in A\cup B\) if and only if \(x\in A\lor x\in B\). By Definition 1.14, \(x\in A\cap B\) if and only if \(x\in A\land x\in B\).

    Proof (i). To prove the first statement, we need to show that every element of \(A\) is also an element of \(A \cup B\):

    \[ \begin{aligned} x \in A &\Rightarrow x \in A \lor x \in B &&\text{by 1.5(i)}\\ &\Rightarrow x \in A \cup B &&\text{by 1.13}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \(A \subseteq A \cup B\) by Definition 1.10.

    Similarly, for \(B \subseteq A \cup B\),

    \[ \begin{aligned} x \in B &\Rightarrow x \in A \lor x \in B &&\text{by 1.5(i)'}\\ &\Rightarrow x \in A \cup B &&\text{by 1.13}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \(B \subseteq A \cup B\) by 1.10.

    Proof (ii). For the second statement, we need to show \(x \in A \cap B \Rightarrow x \in A\).

    \[ \begin{aligned} x \in A \cap B &\Rightarrow x \in A \land x \in B &&\text{by 1.14}\\ &\Rightarrow x \in A &&\text{by 1.5(ii)}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \(A \cap B \subseteq A\) by 1.10.

    Similarly,

    \[ \begin{aligned} x \in B \cap A &\Rightarrow x \in B \land x \in A &&\text{by 1.14}\\ &\Rightarrow x \in B &&\text{by 1.5(ii)}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \(B \cap A \subseteq B\) by 1.10.

    Note: The book prints \(B \cap B \subseteq B\) in 1.20(ii). This is true, but \(B \cap A \subseteq B\) gives the symmetric statement corresponding to \(A \cap B \subseteq A\).

    Next, we have the following theorem.

    1.21 Theorem

    If \(A\) and \(B\) are classes, then

      1. \(A \subseteq B\) if and only if \(A \cup B = B\).
      1. \(A \subseteq B\) if and only if \(A \cap B = A\).

    Proof (i). First, suppose \(A \subseteq B\). We need to show \(A \cup B = B\).

    \[ \begin{aligned} x \in A \cup B &\Rightarrow x \in A \lor x \in B &&\text{by 1.13}\\ &\Rightarrow x \in B \lor x \in B &&\text{since } A \subseteq B\\ &\Rightarrow x \in B &&\text{by 1.8(iv)}. \end{aligned} \]

    Thus, \(A \cup B \subseteq B\) by 1.10. Also, \(B \subseteq A \cup B\) by 1.20(i). Therefore, \(A \cup B = B\) by 1.11(iv).

    Conversely, suppose \(A \cup B = B\). By 1.20(i), \(A \subseteq A \cup B\). Hence \(A \subseteq B\) by substitution (Axiom A1).

    Therefore,

    \[ A \subseteq B \iff A \cup B = B. \]

    Proof (ii). First, suppose \(A \subseteq B\). By 1.20(ii), \(A \cap B \subseteq A\).

    For the other inclusion,

    \[ \begin{aligned} x \in A &\Rightarrow x \in B &&\text{by 1.10}\\ &\Rightarrow x \in A \land x \in B &&\text{since }x \in A\\ &\Rightarrow x \in A \cap B &&\text{by 1.14}. \end{aligned} \]

    Thus, \(A \subseteq A \cap B\) by 1.10. Consequently, \(A \cap B = A\) by 1.11(iv).

    Conversely, suppose \(A \cap B = A\).

    \[ \begin{aligned} x \in A &\Rightarrow x \in A \cap B &&\text{by Axiom A1}\\ &\Rightarrow x \in A \land x \in B &&\text{by 1.14}\\ &\Rightarrow x \in B &&\text{by 1.5(ii)'}. \end{aligned} \]

    Thus, \(A \subseteq B\) by 1.10, and therefore

    \[ A \subseteq B \iff A \cap B = A. \]

    Next, we have

    1.22 Theorem (Absorption Laws)

    For all classes \(A\) and \(B\),

      1. \(A \cup (A \cap B)=A\).
      1. \(A \cap (A \cup B)=A\).

    These can be proved with the theorems we have just covered.

    Proof (i). By 1.20(ii), \(A \cap B \subseteq A\); therefore, by 1.21(i),

    \[ A \cup (A \cap B)=A. \]

    Proof (ii). By 1.20(i), \(A \subseteq A \cup B\); therefore, by 1.21(ii),

    \[ A \cap (A \cup B)=A. \]

    Next, we have

    1.23 Theorem

    For every class \(A\),

    \[ (A')'=A. \]

    Proof. By Definition 1.19,

    \[ \begin{aligned} x \in (A')' &\Rightarrow x \notin A' &&\text{by 1.19}\\ &\Rightarrow x \in A &&\text{by 1.19}. \end{aligned} \]

    Conversely,

    \[ \begin{aligned} x \in A &\Rightarrow x \notin A' &&\text{by 1.19}\\ &\Rightarrow x \in (A')' &&\text{by 1.19}. \end{aligned} \]

    Thus, \((A')' \subseteq A\) and \(A \subseteq (A')'\). Therefore, \((A')'=A\) by 1.11(iv) (or Axiom A1).

    Next, we have

    1.24 Theorem (De Morgan’s Laws)

    For all classes \(A\) and \(B\),

      1. \((A \cup B)'=A'\cap B'\).
      1. \((A \cap B)'=A'\cup B'\).

    Proof (i). First, by 1.19, the definition of union (1.13), and the logical De Morgan’s law (Exercise 1.1, Question 2(a)),

    \[ \begin{aligned} x \in (A \cup B)' &\Rightarrow x \notin A \cup B &&\text{by 1.19}\\ &\Rightarrow x \notin A \land x \notin B &&\text{by 1.13 and logical De Morgan's law}\\ &\Rightarrow x \in A' \land x \in B' &&\text{by 1.19}\\ &\Rightarrow x \in A'\cap B' &&\text{by 1.14}. \end{aligned} \]

    Conversely,

    \[ \begin{aligned} x \in A'\cap B' &\Rightarrow x \in A' \land x \in B' &&\text{by 1.14}\\ &\Rightarrow x \notin A \land x \notin B &&\text{by 1.19}\\ &\Rightarrow x \notin A \cup B &&\text{by 1.13 and logical De Morgan's law}\\ &\Rightarrow x \in (A\cup B)' &&\text{by 1.19}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \((A \cup B)'=A'\cap B'\) by Axiom A1.

    Proof (ii). To prove the second one, we can use the same definitions and the logical De Morgan’s law (Exercise 1.1, Question 2(b)).

    \[ \begin{aligned} x \in (A\cap B)' &\Leftrightarrow x \notin A\cap B &&\text{by 1.19}\\ &\Leftrightarrow \neg[(x\in A)\land(x\in B)] &&\text{by 1.14}\\ &\Leftrightarrow x\notin A\lor x\notin B &&\text{by logical De Morgan's law}\\ &\Leftrightarrow x\in A'\lor x\in B' &&\text{by 1.19}\\ &\Leftrightarrow x\in A'\cup B' &&\text{by 1.13}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \((A \cap B)'=A'\cup B'\) by Axiom A1.

    Next, we have

    1.25 Theorem

    For all classes \(A\), \(B\), and \(C\), the following are true.

    Commutative Laws

      1. \(A\cup B=B\cup A\).
      1. \(A\cap B=B\cap A\).

    Idempotent Laws

      1. \(A\cup A=A\).
      1. \(A\cap A=A\).

    Associative Laws

      1. \(A\cup(B\cup C)=(A\cup B)\cup C\).
      1. \(A\cap(B\cap C)=(A\cap B)\cap C\).

    Distributive Laws

      1. \(A\cap(B\cup C)=(A\cap B)\cup(A\cap C)\).
      1. \(A\cup(B\cap C)=(A\cup B)\cap(A\cup C)\).

    Proof (i). By the definition of union and the commutative law of disjunction,

    \[ \begin{aligned} x\in A\cup B &\Leftrightarrow x\in A\lor x\in B &&\text{by 1.13}\\ &\Leftrightarrow x\in B\lor x\in A &&\text{by 1.8(i)}\\ &\Leftrightarrow x\in B\cup A &&\text{by 1.13}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \(A\cup B=B\cup A\) by Axiom A1.

    Proof (ii). Similarly,

    \[ \begin{aligned} x\in A\cap B &\Leftrightarrow x\in A\land x\in B &&\text{by 1.14}\\ &\Leftrightarrow x\in B\land x\in A &&\text{by 1.8(i)'}\\ &\Leftrightarrow x\in B\cap A &&\text{by 1.14}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \(A\cap B=B\cap A\) by Axiom A1.

    Proof (iii).

    \[ \begin{aligned} x\in A\cup A &\Leftrightarrow x\in A\lor x\in A &&\text{by 1.13}\\ &\Leftrightarrow x\in A &&\text{by 1.8(iv)}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \(A\cup A=A\) by Axiom A1.

    Proof (iv).

    \[ \begin{aligned} x\in A\cap A &\Leftrightarrow x\in A\land x\in A &&\text{by 1.14}\\ &\Leftrightarrow x\in A &&\text{by 1.8(iv)'}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \(A\cap A=A\) by Axiom A1.

    Proof (v). By the definition of union and associativity of disjunction,

    \[ \begin{aligned} x\in A\cup(B\cup C) &\Leftrightarrow x\in A\lor x\in B\cup C &&\text{by 1.13}\\ &\Leftrightarrow (x\in A)\lor[(x\in B)\lor(x\in C)] &&\text{by 1.13}\\ &\Leftrightarrow [(x\in A)\lor(x\in B)]\lor(x\in C) &&\text{by 1.8(ii)}\\ &\Leftrightarrow x\in A\cup B\lor x\in C &&\text{by 1.13}\\ &\Leftrightarrow x\in(A\cup B)\cup C &&\text{by 1.13}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \(A\cup(B\cup C)=(A\cup B)\cup C\) by Axiom A1.

    Proof (vi). Similarly, by associativity of conjunction,

    \[ \begin{aligned} x\in A\cap(B\cap C) &\Leftrightarrow (x\in A)\land[x\in B\cap C] &&\text{by 1.14}\\ &\Leftrightarrow (x\in A)\land[(x\in B)\land(x\in C)] &&\text{by 1.14}\\ &\Leftrightarrow [(x\in A)\land(x\in B)]\land(x\in C) &&\text{by 1.8(ii)'}\\ &\Leftrightarrow [x\in A\cap B]\land(x\in C) &&\text{by 1.14}\\ &\Leftrightarrow x\in(A\cap B)\cap C &&\text{by 1.14}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \(A\cap(B\cap C)=(A\cap B)\cap C\) by Axiom A1.

    Proof (vii). Using the distributive law of conjunction over disjunction,

    \[ \begin{aligned} x\in A\cap(B\cup C) &\Leftrightarrow (x\in A)\land[x\in B\cup C] &&\text{by 1.14}\\ &\Leftrightarrow (x\in A)\land[(x\in B)\lor(x\in C)] &&\text{by 1.13}\\ &\Leftrightarrow [(x\in A)\land(x\in B)]\lor[(x\in A)\land(x\in C)] &&\text{by 1.8(iii)}\\ &\Leftrightarrow [x\in A\cap B]\lor[x\in A\cap C] &&\text{by 1.14}\\ &\Leftrightarrow x\in(A\cap B)\cup(A\cap C) &&\text{by 1.13}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \(A\cap(B\cup C)=(A\cap B)\cup(A\cap C)\) by Axiom A1.

    Proof (viii). Using the distributive law of disjunction over conjunction,

    \[ \begin{aligned} x\in A\cup(B\cap C) &\Leftrightarrow (x\in A)\lor[x\in B\cap C] &&\text{by 1.13}\\ &\Leftrightarrow (x\in A)\lor[(x\in B)\land(x\in C)] &&\text{by 1.14}\\ &\Leftrightarrow [(x\in A)\lor(x\in B)]\land[(x\in A)\lor(x\in C)] &&\text{by 1.8(iii)'}\\ &\Leftrightarrow [x\in A\cup B]\land[x\in A\cup C] &&\text{by 1.13}\\ &\Leftrightarrow x\in(A\cup B)\cap(A\cup C) &&\text{by 1.14}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \(A\cup(B\cap C)=(A\cup B)\cap(A\cup C)\) by Axiom A1.

    Next, we have

    1.26 Theorem

    For every class \(A\), where \(\mathcal U\) is the universal class and \(\varnothing\) is the empty class,

      1. \(A\cup\varnothing=A\).
      1. \(A\cap\varnothing=\varnothing\).
      1. \(A\cup\mathcal U=\mathcal U\).
      1. \(A\cap\mathcal U=A\).
      1. \(\mathcal U'=\varnothing\).
      1. \(\varnothing'=\mathcal U\).
      1. \(A\cup A'=\mathcal U\).
      1. \(A\cap A'=\varnothing\).

    Proof (i). By 1.17(i), \(\varnothing\subseteq A\). Thus, by 1.21(i), \(\varnothing\cup A=A\). Therefore, by the commutative law 1.25(i),

    \[ A\cup\varnothing=A. \]

    Proof (ii). By 1.17(i), \(\varnothing\subseteq A\). Thus, by 1.21(ii), \(\varnothing\cap A=\varnothing\). Therefore, by 1.25(ii),

    \[ A\cap\varnothing=\varnothing. \]

    Proof (iii). By 1.17(ii), \(A\subseteq\mathcal U\). Therefore, by 1.21(i),

    \[ A\cup\mathcal U=\mathcal U. \]

    Proof (iv). By 1.17(ii), \(A\subseteq\mathcal U\). Therefore, by 1.21(ii),

    \[ A\cap\mathcal U=A. \]

    Proof (v). By the definitions of complement, universal class, and empty class,

    \[ \begin{aligned} x\in\mathcal U' &\Leftrightarrow x\notin\mathcal U &&\text{by 1.19}\\ &\Leftrightarrow \text{false} &&\text{by 1.15}\\ &\Leftrightarrow x\in\varnothing &&\text{by 1.16}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \(\mathcal U'=\varnothing\) by Axiom A1.

    Proof (vi).

    \[ \begin{aligned} x\in\varnothing' &\Leftrightarrow x\notin\varnothing &&\text{by 1.19}\\ &\Leftrightarrow \text{true} &&\text{by 1.16}\\ &\Leftrightarrow x\in\mathcal U &&\text{by 1.15}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \(\varnothing'=\mathcal U\) by Axiom A1.

    Proof (vii).

    \[ \begin{aligned} x\in A\cup A' &\Leftrightarrow (x\in A)\lor(x\in A') &&\text{by 1.13}\\ &\Leftrightarrow (x\in A)\lor(x\notin A) &&\text{by 1.19}\\ &\Leftrightarrow \text{true} &&\text{by the law of excluded middle}\\ &\Leftrightarrow x\in\mathcal U &&\text{by 1.15}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \(A\cup A'=\mathcal U\) by Axiom A1.

    Proof (viii).

    \[ \begin{aligned} x\in A\cap A' &\Leftrightarrow (x\in A)\land(x\in A') &&\text{by 1.14}\\ &\Leftrightarrow (x\in A)\land(x\notin A) &&\text{by 1.19}\\ &\Leftrightarrow \text{false} &&\text{by the law of noncontradiction}\\ &\Leftrightarrow x\in\varnothing &&\text{by 1.16}. \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore, \(A\cap A'=\varnothing\) by Axiom A1.
